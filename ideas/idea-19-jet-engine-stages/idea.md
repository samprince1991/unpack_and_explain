## Caption

Suck, squeeze, bang, blow. Four words explain every jet engine you've flown with, and step 4 is the clever part.

A fan pulls air in. A compressor squeezes it tight. Fuel burns in the middle in one steady flame. Then the hot gas spins a turbine on its way out, and that turbine keeps the fan and compressor turning.

That's how a jet engine works. It's one loop that keeps itself going, and the leftover gas is what pushes you forward.

In 1983, Air Canada Flight 143 lost both engines at 41,000 ft (12,500 m) when its fuel ran out. The crew glided it to a safe landing at Gimli, Canada.
