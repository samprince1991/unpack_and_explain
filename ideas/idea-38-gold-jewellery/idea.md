## Caption

Your 22k gold jewellery is only 91.6% gold, and that's on purpose. Save this for the next time you buy gold.

Pure gold's so soft it scratches and bends. So jewellers melt in a little copper and silver to harden it. In 22 karat, that's just 2 parts in 24.

Then your ring's cast from wax. A wax copy is buried in plaster, a hot kiln melts it out and leaves a ring-shaped hole, and molten gold poured in sets in that exact shape.

Check the stamp inside your ring: 22K916 means 916 parts gold per 1,000. Jewellery with diamonds is mostly 18K750 or 14K585, since harder gold holds stones better.

Source: World Gold Council; Bureau of Indian Standards; Ganoksin

## Verified facts

Checked 2026-11-10.

- Corrections: setup line was 'bends and can't hold a stone'; no WGC or assay source says pure gold can't hold a stone (24k pieces with stones exist), so it became 'scratches and bends' (bullion dealers: 'very soft and malleable, so not usually used in jewellery'). [BullionByPost](https://www.bullionbypost.co.uk/international/redirect/infopages:infopage/390/)
- 22k = 22/24 = 91.6% gold; the other 8.4% is mostly copper and silver (sometimes zinc), the copper-to-silver balance sets the colour; a 22k jewellery-alloy patent gives 91.6-92% gold by weight. [BlueStone](https://blog.bluestone.com/what-is-22-karat-gold-understanding-purity-composition/), [patent](https://ipiq.io/patents/3241176). Secondary but consistent; say 'copper and silver', not exact percentages.

- 24 carat is pure gold, usually too soft for everyday jewellery; 18 carat = 75% gold + 25% other metals, often copper or silver. 24k 'Chuk Kam' jewellery is typical in China, so say 'most', not 'all'. [World Gold Council](https://www.gold.org/about-gold/about-gold-jewellery)
- Fineness marks: .375 = 9 ct, .417 = 10 ct, .583 = 14 ct (commonly stamped 585), .750 = 18 ct, .833 = 20 ct. US legal minimum is 10 ct; UK, France, Ireland, Austria and Portugal allow 9 ct. [World Gold Council, assay](https://www.gold.org/jewellery/technology/assay/index.html)
- 18k is 75% gold, the rest usually copper and silver; copper gives rose gold its colour. [The Perth Mint](https://www.perthmint.com/news/jewellery/buying-guides/how-is-rose-gold-made/)
- India's BIS hallmark grades include 22K916 (91.6% gold), 18K750 and 14K585; since 1 July 2021 a hallmark has three parts: the BIS mark, purity (carat + fineness) and a 6-digit HUID. [BIS blog, Hallmarking of gold jewellery](https://services.bis.gov.in/php/BIS_2.0/BISBlog/?p=560)
- WGC, Jewellery demand and trade (India gold market series): 'Studded diamond jewellery is predominantly sold in lower caratages (14-carat and 18-carat)'; 'lower carat studded diamond jewellery is typically harder and so lends itself to stone-setting better than softer alloys'. [WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/jewellery-demand-and-trade-india-gold-market-series/17660)
- WGC (Jan 2023, via Business Insider India): thinner, delicate designs are easier in harder, lower-carat alloys, which works better for setting stones; Indian buyers still prefer 22k plain jewellery. [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/finance/news/brides-buy-half-of-all-gold-jewellery-sold-in-india-says-world-gold-council-report/articleshow/97168432.cms)
- Lost-wax casting: the wax pattern is invested in a flask; burnout holds at or below 300 °F (150 °C) for 30+ min (steam pushes the wax out), then about 1,350 °F (730 °C) to cure the investment; metal then enters the cavity. [Ganoksin, burnout cycles](https://www.ganoksin.com/article/wax-casting-burnout-cycles), [Ganoksin, investment procedure](https://www.ganoksin.com/article/investment-procedure-lost-wax-casting/)
- Metal is forced in by centrifugal spin or vacuum. [Wikipedia, Lost-wax casting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost-wax_casting)
