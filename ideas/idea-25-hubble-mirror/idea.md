## Verified facts

Checked 2026-11-06.

- Hubble's main mirror was the wrong shape, too flat away from its center: about 2.2 microns at the edge, roughly 1/50 of a human hair. The 2.2 µm / 1/50 hair figure is widely reported (NASA's own page says the error was 10 times the allowed tolerance). [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/an-ingenius-fix-for-hubbles-famously-flawed-vision), [19FortyFive](https://www.19fortyfive.com/?p=167053)
- Cause: a lens in the null corrector, the tool that checked the mirror's curve while it was polished, was 1.3 mm out of place. Other tests showed the flaw, but their data was rejected. [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/hubbles-mirror-flaw), [Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/space-exploration/techno-turkey-remembering-hubbles-vision-troubles-30-years-on/)
- Fix: COSTAR, 'glasses' for the instruments, plus the new WFPC2 camera, installed in December 1993 on Servicing Mission 1 (STS-61). [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/hubbles-mirror-flaw)
- Cost at launch: $1.5 billion is the most-cited figure (EBSCO says about $1.6B). No NASA primary source was found, so on screen say 'about $1.5 billion'. [EBSCO](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/history/nasa-launches-hubble-space-telescope), [Space.com](https://space.com/15892-hubble-space-telescope.html)
