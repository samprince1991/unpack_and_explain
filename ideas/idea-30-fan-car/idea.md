## Caption

In 1978, a Formula 1 car won with a giant fan on its back. Then it never raced again. Save this one for race day.

Brabham said the fan was for cooling. It wasn't only that. It sucked air out from under the car, and sliding skirts sealed the sides. Less air underneath means lower pressure, so the air above pushed the car into the track. The faster the engine spun, the harder it pulled.

Niki Lauda won the Swedish Grand Prix by over half a minute. Rival teams protested, Brabham withdrew the car, and fan cars weren't allowed after that.

Source: Wikipedia, Brabham BT46; Motor Sport magazine

## Verified facts

Checked 2026-11-10.

- BT46B fan: driven by 'a complex series of clutches running from the engine to a large single fan at the back'; it drew air from under the chassis, sliding skirts sealed the sides; it also drew air through a horizontal radiator, and Brabham 'claimed that this was the primary effect'. [Wikipedia, Brabham BT46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brabham_BT46)
- Niki Lauda won the Swedish GP at Anderstorp on 17 June 1978 'by over half a minute from Riccardo Patrese'. [Wikipedia, Brabham BT46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brabham_BT46), [Motor Sport](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/special-article/the-75-greatest-grands-prix/6/75-1978-swedish-gp-brabham-fan-car-wins-its-one-and-only-race/)
- Rival teams protested; Brabham voluntarily withdrew it; the win stood ('not considered to have been illegal when it raced'); afterwards the CSI declared fan cars would not be allowed. [Wikipedia, Brabham BT46](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brabham_BT46)
- Lower pressure under a body than over it gives a net force toward the low side: [NASA Glenn, pressure](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/pressure.html)
